Turn 1 · Dark mode · proposal
Two dark modes, because the navy can't be both brand and background
1a keeps the navy and pushes it darker, so today's navy cards become the raised surface — continuous with the brand, cheap to ship. 1b drops the navy for a neutral near-black tuned for a car at night: dimmer, warm-shifted, no pure white. Both need the same structural change — cobalt can't be text on dark (2.3:1), so the accent lifts.
Five rules, shared by both
01 · Don't invert
Light mode's ink ramp flipped gives muddy greys. Dark mode is its own ramp, authored against its own base.
02 · Elevation is lighter, not shadowed
Shadows are invisible on dark. A card sits above the page by being a step lighter — three steps, no more.
03 · Status text lifts, status fills don't
Clear and heavy fail as text on dark (4.1:1 and 3.6:1). They stay put as dots and fills; the text gets a lifted twin.
04 · Tints become the dark twin
The pale notice grounds don't survive. Each tint gets a dark counterpart carrying the lifted ink at full opacity.
05 · Never pure white on a big field
#FFFFFF body copy on near-black halates. ink-on-dark #EEF2FB is already the token; white is reserved for the hero number.
